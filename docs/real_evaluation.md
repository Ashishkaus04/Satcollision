# Three-scenario comparison on real orbital data

_Generated by `scripts/run_real_evaluation.py`. Propagation window starts 2026-09-10T07:58:00+00:00; median element-set age at the start 0.6 days; collision-probability model: circular._

## Method, and what is real in it

Every orbit, miss distance, time-to-closest-approach and collision probability below comes from a real Starlink catalogue (10,713 objects), propagated with SGP4 across a 168-hour window at a 30-second step and screened at 5 km. Detection lead time is measured inside a rolling 72-hour screening horizon, the window inside which operators actually screen conjunctions and decide on maneuvers.

The one assigned element is **ownership**: satellites are split between two operators by NORAD catalogue number parity (even -> Operator-A, odd -> Operator-B); catalogue numbers follow launch order, so parity is uncorrelated with orbital shell and does not bias which encounters cross the boundary. This is necessary and is stated rather than hidden: genuine cross-operator threshold crossings are rare enough that no useful distribution can be built from them (see the second run below for what a real catalogue actually contains). Catalogue numbers track launch order, so parity is uncorrelated with orbital shell; splitting by altitude instead would have drawn the boundary along a line the orbits themselves respect, and produced an empty result by construction.

Because cooperation flags an encounter as soon as it enters that horizon, the cooperative lead time saturates at 72 hours; the quantity that actually varies across encounters — and therefore the one the figure plots — is how much later no-cooperation gets there.

A caveat worth keeping in the text: full-sharing and federated produce the *same* detection lead time by design — in both, each operator tracks its own asset at full precision and learns of the encounter in time to act. That equality is an architectural property, not a measurement. What the real data measures is the quantity that genuinely varies: how much warning no-cooperation loses across real encounter geometries.

## Headline result

Of 23,712 screened conjunctions, 11,835 crossed the assigned operator boundary and **312** crossed the operational risk threshold (Pc >= 1e-04).

Of those, 75 are excluded and **237** scored: an encounter whose closest approach falls inside the first 72 hours of the propagation window has its warning time clipped by when the window opened rather than by tracking quality, identically in every scenario, so it carries no information about what cooperation buys.

| Scenario | Median detection lead time | Raw trajectory data exposed | Cross-operator signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| No Cooperation | 22.7 h | 0% | no |
| Full Data Sharing | 72.0 h | 100% | yes |
| **Federated (proposed)** | **72.0 h** | **0%** | yes |

Across those 237 real encounters, cooperation bought a median of **49.3 hours** of extra warning (mean 49.3 h), at zero exposure of raw trajectory data. No-cooperation's own warning time ranged from 19.3 to 26.1 hours. Every encounter was eventually detected under no-cooperation, just later.

Median miss distance across the set: 785 m.

## The negative result this rests on

The same pipeline across Starlink x Kuiper — 11,104 real objects, genuine ownership, nothing assigned — over the same 168-hour window found **0 threshold crossings** out of 30 cross-operator close approaches, the closest passing 1573 m apart (highest Pc 5.7e-06).

This is a real property of today's sky rather than a limitation of the method: megaconstellations are deliberately placed in separated altitude shells, so genuine cross-operator threshold crossings are rare events. It is also the reason a federated system has to be evaluated on an assigned boundary today — and the reason such a system matters as more operators occupy overlapping shells.

## Reproducing this

```bash
PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/run_real_evaluation.py \
    --catalog data/starlink.tle Starlink \
    --cross data/kuiper.tle Kuiper \
    --hours 168
```

TLE files are republished daily, so re-running will produce a different (equally real) set of encounters. `docs/figures/real_evaluation.json` holds the exact data behind this document.
